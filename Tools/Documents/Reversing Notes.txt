0070DFF4 - Blowfish Encrypt()
0070E07C - Blowfish Decrypt()


0070E264 - XORMask Decrypt()
0070E120 - XORMask Encrypt()
0090FBA0 - XORMask Table[];

00704D50 - RecvPacket()


- WriteFile() calls (used with IO Completion Port)
00706051
007032ED
00808DC6

- ReadFile() do an Xref for offsets is used to Send packets via IO Completion Port

You can Trace the XORMask Table and Blowfish Initialization back to any of the
Packet handlers and watch how the packets are dealt with.

Notice there are 2 "sockets areas" used. one in the 00800000 offset range for the INFOServer
another (or 2 or so) in the 00700000 for Login/Game Servers.


OPCODES
Here are the 2 functions used for debug output when send/recv packets are done.
If you do an XREF trace (in IDA) on these addresses, you will see 83+6 recv and 58 send calls
totaling the 147 types of packets possible. When you trace back to a function you will see
a PUSH 38000h in green (or w/e number the ocpode is), this is a hardcoded value.
That is the opcode number. It may variate..
It should be at least 4 digits and within 2-3 lines before the call to the function.
For the Packet Name, scroll up or down and look for a hardcoded string (they are gray)
Write down anything that resembles a name.
Example  "-[ UCC_Eternal::NotifyFreeReload ] ..."
This packet is obviously named "NotifyFreeReload"

Follow each refrenced call and get names if possible as well as opcode number.
This will give you a list of possible packets so you know what to sniff and how much
there is to reverse. Its your light at the end of the packet tunnel!
